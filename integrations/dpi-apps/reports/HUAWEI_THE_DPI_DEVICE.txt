  The single most important field in that capture is Cloudflare's own
  Ack=1612. It is Cloudflare's TCP stack stating: "I sent 4889 bytes and I
  received 1612." 1612 is exactly the length of the ClientHello flight. So the
  client's post-ClientHello bytes were destroyed somewhere on the path, and
  Cloudflare sat waiting for a handshake that could never complete until its own
  15-second handshake timeout expired and it closed the socket itself.

  THE FILTER IS NOT USING A TIMER. Test: complete the TLS handshake, then send
  NOTHING and just sit there.

    [CMD] SSLObject handshake, then idle 30 s, blocked SNI
    [OUT] handshake COMPLETED at t=0.121s
          -> connection CLOSED at t=15.166s, zero bytes received
    [CMD] same test, allowed SNI (workers.dev) on the SAME edge IP
    [OUT] handshake COMPLETED at t=0.114s
          -> 474 bytes received at t=15.126s (normal post-handshake traffic)

  Both events land at ~15 s, and that is the giveaway: the 15-second hang you
  see is CLOUDFLARE'S HANDSHAKE TIMEOUT, not a censorship timer. The filter
  simply never forwards your Finished bytes. It sends no signal of any kind.

  TIME DECISION WINDOW: classification at t=3.098310 (ClientHello out), first
  discarded segment at t=3.217890. The kill takes effect inside 119 ms and is
  completely invisible to the client.
